# Scope note, 12 October 2026

Every verdict counted in 20 of 162 is pairwise and descriptive: it says whether one gap is larger than its benchmark's sampling noise at 95%, taken on its own. The comparisons with first place in the opening example and in finding three are the same kind of check, read one pair at a time. None of it is a simultaneous claim about a whole leaderboard. Across 118 adjacent pairs each tested at 95%, a few separations would be expected by chance alone, so 9 separated pairs do not mean any table is in the right order. The rule for choosing comparisons was fixed before any score was read: every gap quoted in the six launch posts and every adjacent pair on the nine named leaderboard snapshots. Which pairs that rule picks still depends on the ranking in each snapshot, so the leaderboard half of the count is tied to those snapshots. The snapshot identifiers and the full pair list are in the repo (results.csv and data/), so the same set can be rebuilt after the live leaderboards change. A reader raised this on DEV; it is added as a dated note and no numbers changed.
